Data Footprint

Your digital footprint is data about you.

You may be fine sharing your gender or birth date with websites, but how about your face? A digital footprint is the collection of personal information that websites and other online services gather and store about you. It can include information you enter yourself, as well as details collected while you browse, use an app, or interact with a service.

Data you share with sites is effectively impossible to erase once it’s been shared to third parties. Antimalware suites can include features which scan the web for your personal information, and you can contact individual sites to remove it. However, this is time-consuming and doesn’t remove information that has already been passed on.

The best practice is to limit the amount of information about you stored on the web.

Active and passive footprints.

Your digital footprint can be divided into two broad categories:

  • An active footprint is information you knowingly share or allow a service to access. Examples include posting on social media, filling in a profile, or choosing to allow non-essential cookies.
  • A passive footprint is information a service collects as you use it, sometimes without an obvious action from you. This can include your IP address, browsing activity, or location data.

These categories can overlap – a service may collect information you provide directly and additional information about how you use it.

Limit the information you leave behind.

You cannot always control where information goes after it’s been shared. A post may be copied or forwarded, and data held by a service may also be retained or shared with third parties. These steps can help reduce how much information you disclose:

  • Disable non-essential cookies on websites you visit. Some cookies are needed for basic site functions, but usually tracking cookies can be disabled. Browsing in incognito prevents websites from storing cookies, although it makes things more inconvenient by disabling search history and login saving. For some things, it’s a worthwhile tradeoff.
  • Be cautious about social media. Before posting personal details, photos, or your location, consider who might see them. Other people can copy or share information beyond the audience you intended.
  • Limit site and app permissions. Review access to your microphone, camera, location, and contacts. Only grant these permissions when they are needed, and revoke them later if they’re no longer necessary.
  • Create fewer online accounts. Each account gives another service some information about you, such as your email address or profile details. Avoid accounts you do not need, and leave optional profile fields blank.
  • Remove information you no longer need. Delete old accounts you no longer use and securely destroy sensitive files or records you no longer need. Deleting an account or file may not remove copies already shared with other services, but it can help remove some of your footprint.
