Emergency Response

Recognise when a device or account may be compromised.

It is impossible to guarantee that you will never experience a cyberattack. Knowing how to recognise possible signs of a compromise, and what to do if it happens, can help you respond quickly and limit the damage.

This infographic from Irma Šlekytė at NordVPN outlines possible warning signs (2025). The infographic specifies a phone, although these signs apply to any device. None of these signs proves on its own that a device or account has been hacked, but several unexplained signs together are a good reason to investigate.

Look for signs of a possible compromise.

  • Suspicious activity: You notice apps you did not install, calls or messages you did not send, or purchases you did not make.
  • Slower performance, a hard-working fan, or unusual battery drain: Malicious software may be running in the background, such as software that uses your device to mine cryptocurrency. An older device may struggle to keep up with modern software, so this is not a reliable sign by itself.
  • Unusually high data or internet use: Malicious software could use your connection to send spam or carry out other activity. Check for normal causes too, such as downloads or app updates.
  • Unexpected behaviour: Your device restarts unexpectedly, or apps that used to work begin freezing or refusing to open.
  • Excessive pop-ups, ads, or notifications: These may be designed to pressure you into clicking a link or installing something. Do not interact with suspicious messages or notifications.

What to do if you think you have been hacked.

The Canadian Broker Network recommends taking steps quickly to limit further damage:

  • Secure your accounts. Change affected passwords as soon as possible, starting with important accounts such as your email. Use a device you believe is safe, and use each new password only once. If the service allows it, sign out of all other devices or sessions.
  • Protect your finances. Review your bank, credit card, and other financial accounts for unfamiliar activity. Contact your bank or card provider promptly to freeze or secure an affected account and report unauthorized purchases.
  • Check and correct account details. Review recovery information, shipping addresses, payment details, and other settings for changes you did not make. Look for accounts opened using your email address or other personal information, and contact the relevant service about anything unfamiliar.
  • Warn your contacts. Let friends, family, and other contacts know that the account was compromised, and ask them not to click suspicious links or respond to unusual requests from it.
  • Secure the device. After addressing urgent account and financial issues, update your operating system and apps and run a reputable security scan. If the device appears to be fully compromised, seek professional help or reinstall the operating system, then restore only from a backup you trust.
