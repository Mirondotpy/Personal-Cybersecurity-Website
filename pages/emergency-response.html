Emergency Response

Recognise when a device or account may be compromised.

It is impossible to guarantee that you will never experience a cyberattack. Knowing how to recognise possible signs of a compromise, and what to do if it happens, can help you respond quickly and limit the damage.

This infographic from Irma Šlekytė at NordVPN outlines possible warning signs (2025). The infographic specifies a phone, although these signs apply to any device. None of these signs proves on its own that a device or account has been hacked, but several unexplained signs together are a good reason to investigate.

Look for signs of a possible compromise.

  • Suspicious activity: You notice apps you didn’t install, calls or messages you did not send, or purchases you did not make.
  • Slower performance, a hard-working fan, or unusual battery drain: Malicious software may be running in the background, such as software that uses your device to mine cryptocurrency. A device may struggle regardless, especially if it’s older or running demanding software, so this is not a reliable sign by itself.
  • Unusually high data or internet use: Malicious software could use your connection to send spam or carry out other activity. Check for normal causes too, such as large downloads or app updates.
  • Unexpected behaviour: Your device restarts unexpectedly, or apps that used to work begin freezing or refusing to open.
  • Excessive pop-ups, ads, or notifications: These may be designed to pressure you into clicking a link or installing something. Bad actors sometimes send a flood of messages as you’re going to bed, which is a time when you aren’t as alert.

What to do if you think you have been hacked.

The Canadian Broker Network recommends taking steps quickly to limit further damage:

  • Secure your accounts. Change affected passwords as soon as possible, starting with important accounts such as your email. Use a device you believe is safe, and use each new password only once. If the service allows it, sign out of all other devices or sessions to log the hacker out.
  • Protect your finances. Review your bank, credit card, and other financial accounts for unfamiliar activity. Contact your bank or card provider promptly to freeze or secure an affected account and report unauthorized purchases.
  • Check and correct account details. Review recovery information, shipping addresses, payment details, and other settings for changes you didn’t make. Hackers can change shipping addresses, for example, to mail things to themselves instead of you.
  • Check for new accounts. Look for accounts opened using your email address or other personal information. The attacker might make an account on an online store or gambling site to spend your money quickly.
  • Warn your contacts. Communicate with friends, family, and other contacts to let them know that your account was compromised, and ask them not to click suspicious links or respond to unusual requests from it.
  • Secure the device. After addressing immediate account and financial concerns, improve your device’s security if your negligence led to the attack.
