Passwords

Passwords are your first line of defense for an account.

It's important for them to be secure.

  • Mariusz Michalowski from Spacelift highlights that in 36% of data breaches, attackers used a valid password.
  • On average, people have more than 250 passwords. This is unfeasible to remember, so a good practice is to use a password manager. Make absolutely sure that you’re using a trusted one.
  • That way, you only need to remember one “master password”, from which you can get all your other passwords. Since you’re relying on this one so much, it needs to be very secure – something like 20 characters at least.

The traditional advice on creating a password isn’t very useful nowadays.

  • Modern-day brute force attacks aren't guessed by humans anymore – instead by computer algorithms which churn through tens of billions of passwords a second
  • “At that speed, your clever substitution of “@” for “a” buys you microseconds of additional security” (Bhargava, 2026)
  • Requiring upper and lower case letters, a number, and a special character doesn't make it harder for machines to guess – just harder for humans to remember.
  • The best security measure is length – the longer the better and at least 16 characters.
  • A memorable password format is a “passphrase”, which is a string of 5 to 7 common words strung together, with some added or edited characters. This format is easy to remember but hard to guess.
  • Never use your name, pets’ names, username, or name of the service as part of your password – any of these things are easier to guess than random words.
  • Additionally, make sure to never reuse passwords for two different services, since if a hacker gains access to one service they might try the same password on many others.

Many are looking beyond passwords for security.

A newer method of authentication is a passkey, which is an encrypted digital “key” stored on your computer.

  • Through exchange of a public key, services can authenticate the login while your private key is kept safe in your device’s file system.
  • This puts a lot of responsibility on you. If a hacker gets into your computer, they’d be able to access any of your accounts via your key files.
  • They also aren’t supported on many services, forcing users to use both passwords and passkeys.

The best way to protect an account is multi-factor authentication.

  • Eventually, a determined attacker will be able to get into your account, possibly by hijacking browser cookies or bypassing a password in some other way.
  • Multi-factor authentication uses a password plus one or more additional verification methods, such as a text message or biometrics.
  • Needing two or more pieces of information to log in is less convenient, but makes it a lot harder to break into your account. It’s also not supported everywhere.
  • Multi-factor should be used for important services such as bank accounts, password managers, and email, while it may not be necessary for all accounts.
